ICC Cricket World Cup - Complete Reference Document

=== OVERVIEW ===
The ICC Cricket World Cup is the international championship of One Day International (ODI) cricket.
It is organized by the International Cricket Council (ICC) and held every four years.
It is one of the world's most viewed sporting events, alongside the FIFA World Cup and the Olympics.
The tournament was first held in 1975 in England.

=== FORMAT ===
- Each match is a One Day International (ODI): 50 overs per side
- Teams qualify through ICC rankings and regional qualifiers
- Tournament includes group stage, Super 12s/Super 8s, semi-finals, and final
- Host nation automatically qualifies

=== WORLD CUP WINNERS (ODI) ===

1975 - West Indies
- Venue: England
- Final: West Indies beat Australia by 17 runs
- Player of the Tournament: Clive Lloyd (West Indies)
- Notable: First ever World Cup. Clive Lloyd scored 102 in the final.

1979 - West Indies
- Venue: England
- Final: West Indies beat England by 92 runs
- Notable: Viv Richards scored 138* in the final. West Indies back-to-back champions.

1983 - India
- Venue: England
- Final: India beat West Indies by 43 runs
- Captain: Kapil Dev
- Notable: Biggest upset in cricket history. Kapil Dev's famous 175* vs Zimbabwe in group stage.
- Impact: This win transformed cricket in India and made it the most popular sport in the country.

1987 - Australia
- Venue: India and Pakistan (first time outside England)
- Final: Australia beat England by 7 runs
- Captain: Allan Border
- Notable: First World Cup held outside England.

1992 - Pakistan
- Venue: Australia and New Zealand
- Final: Pakistan beat England by 22 runs
- Captain: Imran Khan
- Notable: Imran Khan's famous "cornered tigers" speech. Wasim Akram's brilliant bowling in the final.

1996 - Sri Lanka
- Venue: India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka
- Final: Sri Lanka beat Australia by 7 wickets
- Captain: Arjuna Ranatunga
- Notable: Sanath Jayasuriya revolutionized opening batting with aggressive play. Aravinda de Silva scored 107* in the final.

1999 - Australia
- Venue: England
- Final: Australia beat Pakistan by 8 wickets
- Captain: Steve Waugh
- Notable: Shane Warne and Glenn McGrath dominated. Pakistan collapsed for 132 in the final.

2003 - Australia
- Venue: South Africa
- Final: Australia beat India by 125 runs
- Captain: Ricky Ponting
- Notable: Ricky Ponting scored 140* in the final. Australia's dominant era. Sachin Tendulkar was top scorer of the tournament.

2007 - Australia
- Venue: West Indies
- Final: Australia beat Sri Lanka by 53 runs (D/L method)
- Captain: Ricky Ponting
- Notable: Australia won three consecutive World Cups (1999, 2003, 2007). Glenn McGrath took 26 wickets.

2011 - India
- Venue: India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh
- Final: India beat Sri Lanka by 6 wickets
- Captain: MS Dhoni
- Notable: MS Dhoni hit the winning six. Sachin Tendulkar's last World Cup. India won on home soil after 28 years.
- Famous Moment: MS Dhoni's helicopter shot six to win the final at Wankhede Stadium, Mumbai.

2015 - Australia
- Venue: Australia and New Zealand
- Final: Australia beat New Zealand by 7 wickets
- Captain: Michael Clarke
- Notable: Mitchell Starc was Player of the Tournament. New Zealand's first ever World Cup final.

2019 - England
- Venue: England and Wales
- Final: England beat New Zealand (Super Over after tie)
- Captain: Eoin Morgan
- Notable: Most dramatic final in World Cup history. Match tied, Super Over tied, England won on boundary count. Ben Stokes scored 84*.

2023 - Australia
- Venue: India
- Final: Australia beat India by 6 wickets
- Captain: Pat Cummins
- Notable: India were unbeaten in the tournament until the final. Travis Head scored 137 in the final. Virat Kohli was Player of the Tournament with 765 runs.

=== LEGENDARY PLAYERS ===

Sachin Tendulkar (India)
- Role: Batsman
- World Cups: 1992, 1996, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2011 (6 World Cups)
- World Cup runs: 2278 runs (most in World Cup history)
- Notable: Called the "God of Cricket". Won the 2011 World Cup in his last attempt.

Ricky Ponting (Australia)
- Role: Batsman / Captain
- World Cups: Won 3 consecutive World Cups as captain (1999 as player, 2003, 2007 as captain)
- Notable: Scored 140* in 2003 final. Most successful World Cup captain.

Wasim Akram (Pakistan)
- Role: Fast Bowler
- Known as: Sultan of Swing
- Notable: Took 2 crucial wickets in 1992 final. One of the greatest fast bowlers ever.

Glenn McGrath (Australia)
- Role: Fast Bowler
- World Cup wickets: 71 (most in World Cup history)
- Notable: Dominated three World Cups. Took 26 wickets in 2007 World Cup.

MS Dhoni (India)
- Role: Wicket-keeper Batsman / Captain
- Notable: Led India to 2011 World Cup victory. Famous for finishing matches with big shots.
- Famous Moment: Winning six in 2011 final.

Virat Kohli (India)
- Role: Batsman
- Notable: 765 runs in 2023 World Cup (Player of the Tournament). Consistent performer across multiple World Cups.

=== RECORDS ===
- Most runs in World Cup history: Sachin Tendulkar - 2278 runs
- Most wickets in World Cup history: Glenn McGrath - 71 wickets
- Highest individual score: Martin Guptill (New Zealand) - 237* vs West Indies, 2015
- Most World Cup titles: Australia - 6 titles (1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2015, 2023)
- Highest team total: Australia - 417/6 vs Afghanistan, 2015
- Lowest team total: Canada - 36 all out vs Sri Lanka, 2003

=== T20 WORLD CUP ===
The ICC T20 World Cup is a separate tournament for Twenty20 International cricket (20 overs per side).
- First held in 2007 in South Africa
- India won the inaugural T20 World Cup in 2007 under MS Dhoni
- India won the 2024 T20 World Cup under Rohit Sharma, defeating South Africa in the final
- West Indies won in 2012 and 2016
- England won in 2010 and 2022
- Pakistan won in 2009
- Sri Lanka won in 2014

=== KEY CRICKET TERMS ===
- Over: 6 balls bowled by one bowler
- Wicket: Dismissal of a batsman
- Century (100): Batsman scores 100 or more runs
- Duck: Batsman dismissed for 0 runs
- Hat-trick: Bowler takes 3 wickets in 3 consecutive balls
- LBW: Leg Before Wicket - ball hits batsman's leg in line with stumps
- No-ball: Illegal delivery, batting team gets extra run
- Wide: Ball bowled too wide for batsman to hit, extra run awarded
- DRS: Decision Review System - technology used to review umpire decisions
- D/L Method: Duckworth-Lewis method - calculates revised targets in rain-affected matches
